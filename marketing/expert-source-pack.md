### 3 ready-to-adapt sample answers

**Q: "Why do people get a surprise tax bill on their RSUs?"**
> Because employers withhold federal tax on RSU vests at a flat 22% (the IRS "supplemental wage"
> rate), but most senior tech workers are actually in the 32-37% bracket. That gap isn't caught
> until you file. The fix is to top up Form W-4 line 4(c) or make a quarterly estimated payment
> before December 31. (Rule: IRS Pub 15-T; Treas. Reg. §31.3402(g)-1.) — [Name], founder of Mathstub.

**Q: "What's the biggest equity-comp tax mistake you see?"**
> Leaving the broker's "$0 cost basis" on a 1099-B for sold RSUs. You already paid income tax on
> those shares when they vested, so your cost basis equals the vest-day value (IRC §1012). If you
> don't correct it on Form 8949, you get taxed twice on the same money — sometimes thousands of
> dollars. — [Name], founder of Mathstub.

**Q: "How are stock options taxed differently from RSUs?"**
> RSUs are taxed as ordinary income at vesting on their full value (IRC §83). NSOs are taxed as
> ordinary income on the spread at exercise. ISOs can be tax-free at exercise for regular tax but
> the spread is an Alternative Minimum Tax preference item (IRC §56), which catches a lot of people
> by surprise. The right move depends heavily on timing. — [Name], founder of Mathstub.
